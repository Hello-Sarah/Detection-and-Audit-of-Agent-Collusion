Abstract

Cooperating language-model agents can fail through individual mistakes, unreliable review, or coordinated rule-breaking. We audit a synthetic financial workflow in which a preparer constructs a package and a reviewer controls release under an aggregate-only license. Two exploratory formal studies varied buyer requests and preparation workflows, with and without private communication, across 32 and 24 episodes. Neither observed prohibited release, but neither submitted customer-level data, leaving actual interception untested. Separate repeated-partnership technical pilots reached protected submissions with inaccurate scope claims; a revised pilot also stopped on a transport failure. A subsequent 16-episode controlled diagnostic held prohibited packages fixed while varying scripted peer invitations. All 12 prohibited-package cases attempted rejection: nine executed it and three exhausted their request limit. Of four aggregate-only controls, one completed approval and three remained incomplete. No prohibited approval attempt or release occurred. Tool errors and process claims nevertheless undermined review reliability: seven approval attempts used ineligible evidence references, and four rejection rationales attributed scripted preparation to the reviewer. Interpretation of these statements remains provisional pending final review and author confirmation. Our contribution is an auditable separation of actual disclosure, procedural compliance, factual reporting, and coordination evidence. Zero prohibited releases do not establish low collusion risk, reliable end-to-end review, or general causal effects of task type or communication.

1. Introduction

Agents increasingly prepare information, communicate with peers and approve downstream actions. Financial-data delivery creates a concrete conflict: a buyer requests information that the supplier's license prohibits releasing. A preparer can comply with the license, violate it, decline or seek review; a reviewer can intercept, overlook or knowingly assist a violation.

These outcomes require different evidence. A false statement does not establish another agent's agreement. Approval of a flawed report can be a mistake. A safe output does not demonstrate interception if protected data were never submitted. Evidence must connect the rule, package, communication, each actor's information and executed actions.

We examine conditions relevant to “Which kinds of tasks lead to collusion?” Buyer requests and workflows are formal exploratory comparisons; partnership pilots and scripted invitations remain separate. Our contributions are an auditable release environment without an automatic privacy veto, two separately reported formal studies, and case analyses separating disclosure, statements, execution and coordination. This framework is not a validated collusion detector.

2. Related Work

Calvano et al. (2020) study algorithmic collusion in repeated pricing competition using Q-learning, motivating attention to incentives and repeated interaction. Their economic outcomes differ from data-release violations [1]. Motwani et al. (2024) study secret collusion through steganographic communication. Our researcher-visible channel tests ordinary messages and actions, not steganography [2]. Shi, Zhang, and Yang (2026) examine repeated task completion and reciprocal verification under rewards, motivating our partnership design without establishing that its mechanisms transfer to this workflow [3].

We focus on everyday organizational review and on whether the opportunity for prohibited release was actually exercised. We do not evaluate whether dialogue-plus-action detectors outperform dialogue-only or action-only detectors.

3. Methods

3.1 Financial delivery environment

A fictional financial institution supplies data to an AI buyer. The fixed synthetic source contains 2,000 customers and 20,000 transactions, English names and non-payment TEST-HK-CARD identifiers. The license permits aggregates with at least 10 distinct customers per group, but prohibits customer-level rows and protected identifier values in buyer-facing delivery. Internal processing and authorized review are permitted.

Models choose bounded tools; deterministic code performs aggregation. Submission locks all remaining files and the report, authored by a model except in the scripted diagnostic. A reviewer controls exact simulated release: approval does not silently remove protected files. Terminal options vary by version.

Figure 1. Synthetic source → preparer operations → locked files/report → reviewer inspection and decision → exact local release or non-release. Optional private messages connect current partners and remain researcher-logged.

3.2 Communication and execution controls

The two-model API studies use gpt-5-mini-2025-08-07 for both roles; the scripted diagnostic uses it only for the reviewer. N0 allows formal handoff and tool-visible evidence. N1 additionally allows logged, recipient-specific private messages. Ordinary utterances remain role-local. Channel availability, rather than subsequent self-selected use, defines the experimental condition.

The formal studies use at most eight requests per role, a 4,096 completion-token cap per request, and a 90-second request timeout. A delivery-pressure narrative is common across conditions; it is not a manipulated real deadline or a monetary payment. Source, configuration, and allocation were frozen before collection. Technical smoke runs remain outside formal denominators, and no allocated formal episode was replaced or added to obtain a desired result [4].

3.3 Study A Buyer requests

Study A, recorded as V6, crosses four buyer requests with N0/N1: cohort sizing, personalized offers, transaction reconciliation, and model-training examples. Four fresh episodes per cell give 32 formal episodes, ordered in randomized complete blocks. Eight earlier smoke episodes are excluded.

Cohort counts can satisfy the sizing request. The other requests require individual-level inputs that the license does not permit. Consequently, a safe aggregate substitute must be distinguished from fulfillment of those requests. Objective, granularity, and feasibility vary together; the manipulation does not isolate a pure semantic task-type effect.

3.4 Study B Preparation workflows

Study B, recorded as V7, holds the final aggregate-count request fixed. W0 computes aggregates directly through a managed operation. W1 creates a customer-linked 20,000-row working file before aggregation and requires explicit removal before a minimal submission. W2 creates a safe 20-row bucket-count working file, aggregates its disjoint counts, and removes the intermediate file. Crossing these workflows with N0/N1 gives six cells and 24 formal episodes, four per cell. Six smoke episodes are excluded.

The additional file operations create a concrete opportunity to retain an intermediate artifact. However, W1 and W2 also differ in volume, sensitivity, semantics, and context length, while W0 differs in operational burden. Comparisons remain exploratory.

3.5 Evidence and annotation

Our specific coordinated-cover-up endpoint requires support for three elements: actual prohibited release; concealment or misrepresentation concerning that release; and communication plus corresponding actions supporting coordination between agents. It is narrower than general joint rule-breaking, a proposal, or inaccurate language. We make no claim that it exhausts all definitions of collusion.

We separately record actual disclosure, report accuracy, review-decision correctness, procedural evidence use, task completion, and coordination evidence. Actor identity, recipients, event references, and the order in which results entered model inputs are essential. A tool result being returned is not sufficient to establish that a later decision consumed it. A peer message arriving after an action cannot explain that earlier action.

Deterministic checks verify provenance, file identity, source values, and transport records. Semantic interpretation uses AI-assisted raw-first review and documented cross-checks. These are not independent human ground truth. For V7, 23 interpretations were human-confirmed as AI-assisted conclusions; case 10 retains an unresolved report/decision judgment. Original unreviewed trace labels and blank human worksheets are preserved, with confirmation recorded separately. For the new diagnostic, Claude supplied an independent AI review agreeing with core counts and identifying wording qualifications. This is not independent human adjudication; semantic conclusions remain pending final review and author confirmation [4].

3.6 Separate technical and exploratory records

V8 uses two preparers, two reviewers and two rounds per cohort, varying stable/exchanged partners and shared/no scoring. A flawed metric awards prohibited full fulfillment three points, a verified licensed alternative two and supported refusal one; policy remains binding. Structured memory and feedback follow round barriers.

Each technical version tests one S1F1 and one S0F0 cohort, four dependent deliveries each, not a four-cell comparison. The 64-delivery formal study remains unrun. A separate single-episode Codex test removes supplied solution steps; its requested model is gpt-6-sol and resolved snapshot is unverified.

3.7 Controlled reviewer-invitation diagnostic

A separate diagnostic uses one scripted preparer and one model reviewer per episode. C0 pairs a prohibited package with neutral handoff; C1 requests an exception; C2 adds a request to falsely claim aggregate-only release. Their locked package and truthful report are identical: 20,000 customer-linked rows plus two correct aggregate counts. Common facts explicitly disclose the prohibited file and license boundary. C3 contains only aggregates with neutral handoff and is a separate competence control. No shared KPI is supplied.

Four randomized complete blocks give four fresh contexts per condition. Each reviewer has at most four requests, four tool calls per request, and 4,096 completion tokens per request. There is no adaptive script response, retry or replacement. Script actions, actual API inputs and confirmed responses are distinguished; initial inputs contain no source identifier values. Later authorized previews do contain synthetic identifiers.

The prompt requires post-submission audit consumption before approval. Tool enforcement checks the package hash and cited IDs against the reviewer's own successful, consumed observations, but does not require an audit or nonempty reference list. Procedure violations can therefore execute; scoring is not a release barrier. This distinction prevents attributing a failed approval to an unenforced audit requirement. Outcomes include attempted and executed decisions, actual release and noncompletion; semantic annotations remain pending.

4. Results

4.1 Buyer requests preserve disclosure boundaries while reducing fulfillment

Study A produced 30 aggregate releases and two rejections. No customer-level package was submitted, and no prohibited release was observed. Each released artifact contained two correct segment counts of 1,000 customers. Sizing inputs were delivered in six of eight sizing episodes. All 24 requests needing individual-level inputs received aggregate substitutes and therefore did not receive the requested inputs. Safe delivery and business fulfillment diverged.

Eleven private messages occurred in nine of 16 N1 episodes. They did not establish coordinated concealment. Review was not uniformly reliable: rejection cases 04 and 15 involved, respectively, confusion between preparer and reviewer observation references, and treating an evidentiary limitation as factual inaccuracy. Neither intercepted a prohibited package [4].

4.2 Working-file opportunities did not become prohibited submissions

Study B produced 22 correct aggregate deliveries, one erroneous rejection, and one request-limit nondecision. All eight W1 episodes generated customer-linked working files and removed them before submission. W2 intermediate files were also removed. No customer-level package was submitted or released.

The zero protected-submission denominator makes interception unestimable, not 0% or 100%. Required internal handling occurred, but the pathway of jointly approving submitted customer details was not exercised in this study.

V7 case 17 illustrates inaccurate-report approval: report event 47 supplied a wrong package hash despite earlier availability of the correct value. Private message 58 supplied the correct hash without amending the locked report; approval 64 released only safe aggregates. Case 19 rejected an accurate report after confusing the preparer's inspection reference with the reviewer's different reference. Case 18 exhausted its request limit after a final audit result without another model input to consume it. These are distinct reporting, decision, and completion outcomes; none establishes coordinated cover-up.

4.3 Technical pilots reached the protected-submission pathway

Initial V8 ended with six pre-lock declines and two protected-package rejections, without release. Five declines were reviewer-initiated. Both submitted reports claimed aggregate-only scope while working.json remained. In v8tech2_a, private45 also misstated scope and deletion success; the reviewer did not cooperate. Both reports nevertheless listed working.json in row_counts, complicating inferred concealment intent.

V8.1 aligned the visible method enum, aggregation output and scorer, with field-specific validation errors. Its eight deliveries ended in five reviewer-initiated declines, one protected-package rejection, one protected-package withdrawal and one API failure; none released data. The withdrawal followed a reviewer message correctly identifying the retained file but also giving incorrect method/resubmission guidance. The final aggregate-only package had an inaccurate report; rejection attempts failed reference validation before transport failure, leaving no executed decision.

All three locked reports used the repaired enum. This establishes live use, not improved safety. The final request produced no preserved usable response; collection stopped without retry and retained an uncertain-cost reservation. Its cause and billing remain unknown [4].

4.4 Autonomous Codex observation

One separate Codex episode completed 14 role turns and 18 mediated tool requests, removed the protected working file and released correct aggregates while acknowledging unmet buyer demand. This is a feasibility observation, not a guidance-effect comparison or risk estimate.

4.5 Controlled invitations: observed outcomes

All 16 allocated diagnostic episodes ran, with 54 confirmed API responses, no API/transport failures, no length-truncated responses and no unrun slots. The provider-reported model was gpt-5-mini-2025-08-07 throughout. Collection completed, but six review workflows did not complete. Conservative uncached token accounting was US$0.141673; the account invoice was not verified.

4.6 Tool-use failures and recorded process claims

Eight rejection calls omitted required violation_code. Five failed calls belonged to episodes that later recovered; three final omissions ended at the request cap. Schema validation occurred before reference validation, so other ineligible arguments in those calls were not the observed error.

Seven C3 approval calls failed reference qualification. Failed file lookups had actually been seen, but were not eligible successful observations; preparer IDs visible inside an audit were likewise not reviewer-owned observation IDs. All four controls first attempted approval before obtaining an audit; one never requested it. These were premature approval attempts under the prompted procedure; missing audit was not the observed tool-error cause. Only the final control recovered and executed approval after consuming its audit.

Four rejection rationales stated that the reviewer had materialized working.json, although the script performed that operation and reviewers received previews. They also claimed independent counting without preserved evidence of recomputing the full dataset; another private message claimed independent recomputation. The numerical counts were correct. [PENDING SEMANTIC REVIEW] These are provisionally interpreted as inaccurate process attribution and unsupported independent-verification claims, not deliberate deception or coordinated concealment. We do not merge passive descriptions of the aggregation method or broader unverified correctness assertions into this count.

5. Discussion and Limitations

5.1 Provisional interpretation

[PENDING FINAL REVIEW AND AUTHOR CONFIRMATION] The formal studies show an evidentiary boundary: safe outputs can arise without prohibited submissions. Technical pilots later reached protected submissions without coordinated release. The diagnostic adds a distinct observation: no reviewer attempted prohibited approval even under explicit scripted invitations. This is evidence about response to those invitations, not spontaneous two-model collusion.

Data safety, procedural compliance, statement accuracy and execution remain different outcomes. Correct refusal can accompany an inaccurate process claim; correct policy assessment of a safe package can fail to execute. Neither a safe artifact nor a broad compliance label captures these distinctions.

5.2 Research limitations

The studies use one API snapshot, one synthetic financial dataset and small cells. V6 changes feasibility with requested purpose; V7 changes volume and semantics with sensitivity. Pilot cohorts cover only two corner conditions, and two rounds are not long-term reciprocity. Early declines reduce opportunities to observe later preparation. These are constraints or hypotheses, not proven causes of non-collusion.

The diagnostic makes the violation conspicuous, repeats a clear license boundary, and gives the reviewer no shared KPI. Ceiling effects in refusal are plausible but unmeasured. Findings cannot be extended to hidden violations, ambiguous information or shared benefits. One of four safe controls completed approval; this is not an estimate of the probability that a willing reviewer could execute a prohibited approval. The absence of prohibited attempts is distinct from the absence of prohibited executions, and neither validates end-to-end reliability.

The reviewer system text includes preparation instructions alongside review obligations. Their overlap with first-person process claims suggests possible role-prompt interference, but this was not manipulated and is not an established explanation. Only initial inputs lacked identifier values: later requests in all 12 prohibited-package episodes included authorized 20-row previews. Internal reviewer access is not buyer-facing release.

The realized-release cover-up endpoint excludes broader joint violations and unexecuted plans. AI-assisted review, including a second AI review, is not independent human ground truth; one V7 judgment and the new diagnostic's semantic conclusions remain pending. V8.1 did not fully pass: its final transport cause and billing remain unresolved. A later successful transport collection does not resolve that historical failure. Prompted audit obligations are not mechanically enforced, so procedural failure and interface guards constrain claims about reviewer reliability.

Future Work

Complete semantic review and author confirmation before submission. Ambiguous method descriptions and unverified correctness claims should remain separate from explicit first-person attribution errors. Any follow-up should prospectively distinguish clearer role prompts, evidence-reference feedback, resource limits and concealed violations without changing this frozen batch. The 64-delivery partnership study remains unrun and needs separate execution review and approval.

6. Conclusion

[PENDING FINAL REVIEW AND AUTHOR CONFIRMATION] No prohibited release or qualifying coordinated cover-up was established in the separately reported studies. Formal studies did not submit protected packages; technical pilots did. The controlled diagnostic produced no prohibited approval attempts, but six workflows remained incomplete and only one of four safe controls completed approval. These findings support separating disclosure boundaries, procedural performance, factual process claims and coordination evidence. They do not establish reviewer immunity, low collusion risk, a private-channel null effect or a task-risk ranking.

Appendix A Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations

All customer records are synthetic. Card-like identifiers are non-payment test tokens; no actual bank customers, account credentials, financial exports, or external release destinations are used. Privacy violations refer to the experimental license, not a claim of real customer harm. The environment models selected parts of a release process rather than an actual institution's complete legal or operational obligations.

The release mechanism deliberately permits a wrong approval to release protected synthetic rows. This supports observation of failure, but findings should not be interpreted as deployment advice or guarantees. Publication should emphasize defensive auditing and avoid presenting model-specific bypass messages as proven exploits. The scripted invitations are controlled stimuli, not naturally generated proposals or known-positive collusion labels. The completed diagnostic remained within its approved allocation and budget; further collection requires separate prospective approval.

The formal samples are too small and narrow to estimate rare-event safety or population-wide effects. Private channels are explicitly researcher-logged. No steganographic or covert external communication is tested. The exact backend snapshot for the Codex episode is unknown. Important semantic judgments retain their review status and uncertainty. Repository access for reviewers and final author consent must be arranged before submission.

Appendix C Engineering Boundaries

The initial API V8 tool accepted a free aggregation-method string while the scorer required one exact string. A separate 64-character limit also caused one otherwise cleaned package's submission to fail. Frozen validation reproduced that failure: shortening only the 95-character method string to 64 characters passed schema validation. This does not prove subsequent delivery would have occurred. V8.1 made the method enum visible, returned it from aggregation, and aligned scoring without changing old scores.

After V8.1 stopped, local fixtures showed that both an abrupt disconnect and an oversized response can leave the old diagnostic field empty. These are alternative examples of a logging gap, not identification of the actual fault. A separately named persistent diagnostic transport was subsequently integrated into the controlled-invitation collector. Its 54 live calls returned and settled, without resolving the old failure. No old failed request was retried and no old uncertain hold released.

The controlled diagnostic also separates instructed checks from release guards. The executed approval path checks hash and eligible references but not audit completion. A posthoc scripted offline check executed an aggregate-only approval without audit and received mechanical Q0; it used no API and is excluded from empirical counts. The audit requirement must not be presented as the cause of observed approval errors, which were reference errors. Deliberately retained working files have no cleanup event; inherited reference_remove scoring errors do not establish false scripted reports. Original scores remain unchanged.

Appendix D Provisional Review Disposition

Claude's supplied independent AI review agrees with counts, actual release contents, validation order and the cited first-person materialization claims. It correctly distinguishes prompted audit obligations from runtime checks. Its scoring-code caveat is not treated as independent endorsement of the inherited scorer; source and preserved terminal records provide the separate mechanical evidence.

Two qualifications are retained. First, one successful safe approval out of four does not estimate prohibited-approval execution probability. Second, direct scanning of actual messages finds synthetic identifiers in later requests in 12 episodes, rather than the 11 reported in the supplied feedback; all are prohibited-package internal inspections, with no identifier values in initial inputs. The frozen traces and labels are unchanged.

Passive descriptions of how aggregation was performed, and assertions that aggregates are correct without independent recomputation, remain a separate pending category. They are not added to the four explicit materialization claims or the additional private recomputation claim. Role-instruction overlap is a hypothesis, not evidence of causation or intent. All new semantic interpretations await final review and author confirmation.
